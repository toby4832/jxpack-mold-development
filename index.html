Mold development process: what happens at each step

01
Within 24 hours

Inquiry and requirement brief

You send us the basics: which product the packaging holds, target capacity, material direction, destination market, and expected order volume. Our team replies with a first structural recommendation and an indicative budget range, so you can decide whether the project is worth pursuing before spending anything. A complete brief at this stage prevents costly rework later — for example, the target capacity decides mold cavity size, and the destination market decides which material and decoration pass local compliance such as REACH or FDA.

If you already have a reference photo, a sketch, or a competitor sample, share it — a visual reference shortens the whole development cycle. Every brief is handled under mutual NDA, and our team returns a first recommendation within 24 hours, so you can scope budget before committing any tooling.

What we need from you

What you receive

02
2–3 business days

Feasibility review and quotation

Our engineers check the details that decide whether a design can actually be manufactured: wall thickness, draft angle, thread geometry, filling line compatibility, and how the closure seals. We then advise whether an existing mold can be adapted or a new private mold is required. We also flag risk early: very thin walls may warp, an aggressive draft angle may stick in the cavity, and an unusual neck finish may need a custom closure. Catching these in engineering saves weeks downstream.

You receive a formal quotation covering tooling cost, unit price at different volumes, MOQ, and a stage-by-stage timeline. The quotation is itemised, not a lump sum — you see exactly what the mold, sampling, and per-unit cost look like at 10k, 50k, and 100k pieces, which makes volume planning straightforward.

What we need from you

About 1 week

Design, drawings, and 3D modeling

We produce technical drawings and photorealistic 3D renderings of your packaging. This is where the silhouette, neck finish, shoulder curve, and fill line get locked down. Capacity is finalised in the same stage, whether you need a mainstream size or something outside our standard capacity range. Drawings follow a standard your contract manufacturer can read directly, so the mold we cut matches the part you approve — no silent interpretation gaps. If you are unsure which size fits your formula and fill volume, our team can recommend a standard size that already has a proven mold, which shortens lead time.

04
2–3 days

3D printed prototype

Screens never tell you how a bottle feels in the hand. We print a physical prototype so you can check proportion, grip, and shelf presence before any tooling begins. Changes at this stage cost nothing — changes after tooling starts do. We typically print at 1:1 scale, including the closure, so the proportions you judge are the proportions you will sell. This is the cheapest point in the whole project to change the shape, add a finger grip, or adjust the shoulder. Most brands run one round of prototype feedback; it is normal to tweak wall thickness or the cap interface here, so the steel is cut only once.

What we need from you

  • Any final shape adjustments
What you receive

05
About 3 weeks

Mold development and tooling

Once drawings are signed off, our R&D engineers cut your custom mold. Tooling method depends on your packaging format: injection molding for caps and closures, and thick-wall jars; injection stretch blow molding for PET bottles; extrusion blow molding for HDPE and LDPE containers. Cavity count is set by your volume plan. Steel grade is chosen by expected lifetime and volume: pre-hardened steel for runs up to a few hundred thousand, hardened steel for long programs. Cavity layout is balanced so every cavity fills identically, which keeps dimensional spread tight across the whole shot.

Decoration is engineered into the same stage — if you plan to use hot stamping, silk screen printing, or spray coating, tell us now so the surface geometry supports it. Browse our decoration and finishing processes before this step. Surface finish for decoration is built into the tool where possible — a textured band or a flat landing for hot stamping is cut directly, rather than added later, so registration stays consistent across the run.

What we need from you
  • Signed-off technical drawings
  • Confirmation of decoration method
  • Volume plan for cavity layout
What you receive
  • Completed production mold
  • Tooling schedule updates
  • Maintenance and ownership terms
06
About 1 week

T1 sample, color matching, and revision

The first trial shots come off the mold as T1 samples. We measure dimensions, test closure fit and sealing, and run a filling check. This is also when color gets confirmed — we match to any Pantone reference with a reproduction rate above 95%. Color is signed off against a physical master; we record the formula so repeat orders match the first one, and dimensional reports are shared with you, not buried.

If your packaging needs a pump, sprayer, dropper, or specific liner, we fit the accessories and closures at this stage and test the assembled unit. If the first shot is not right, we revise the mold and produce T2 samples — the loop repeats until the sealed, filled, and drop tests all pass. You only pay for tooling once.

What we need from you

  • Sample evaluation feedback

What you receive

Which route fits your project

Stock mold or private mold

The single biggest decision in any cosmetic packaging development is whether to adapt a mold we already own or build a new one. Here is how the two routes compare.

Route A

Adapt an existing mold

We maintain more than 200 sets of mature molds covering market-proven shapes and mainstream capacities. Adapting one removes tooling cost entirely and cuts weeks off your launch. Best for fast timelines, smaller volumes, and brands validating a new product line.

Route B

Develop a private mold

A private mold gives you an exclusive form no competitor can buy. Tooling is a one-time cost, the shape belongs to your brand, and unit price drops as volume grows. Best for established lines, brand differentiation, and volumes from 10,000 pieces.

Tooling methods

Molding processes we run in-house

Different packaging formats need different tooling — a PET cosmetic bottle and a thick-wall jar are not made the same way. Matching the process to your container is what keeps wall thickness consistent and closures sealing properly.

Injection molding

Used for caps, closures, and thick-wall cosmetic jars. Delivers tight dimensional tolerance and crisp detail for threaded necks and tamper-evident features.

Injection stretch blow molding

The standard route for PET cosmetic bottles. Produces clear, lightweight containers with good barrier properties for serums, lotions, and toners.

Extrusion blow molding

Suited to HDPE and LDPE bottles for larger volumes and household formats, including round, oval, and handled shapes.

Glass mold production

For premium skincare and fragrance. Supports flint, frosted, and amber glass with custom embossing and heavy-base designs.

Common questions

Mold development FAQ

How much does a custom cosmetic packaging mold cost?

Tooling cost depends on structure complexity, cavity count, and steel grade. A simple adaptation of an existing mold may carry no tooling fee at all, while a multi-cavity private mold for a complex bottle is a larger one-time investment. We quote the exact figure at the feasibility stage, before you commit to anything.

How long does mold development take?

Roughly three weeks from signed-off drawings to T1 sample for a private mold. If an existing mold can be adapted, first samples typically arrive within 7–10 days. Full project timeline including design and approvals is usually five to seven weeks.

What is the MOQ for a private mold project?

Private mold projects start at 10,000 pieces. Adapting an existing mold can start from 1,000 pieces for stock colors. Both routes scale with volume, and unit price decreases at higher tiers.

Can you modify an existing mold instead of building a new one?

Often yes. With 200+ stock molds we can frequently adjust neck finish, color, decoration, or capacity without new tooling. We will tell you honestly if your design needs a private mold instead.

Who owns the mold after development?

The mold belongs to you once tooling is paid. We maintain it free of charge while it stays in production with us, and it is reserved exclusively for your orders.

Can you sign an NDA before I share my design?

Yes. We sign mutual NDAs before any drawings or concepts are exchanged, and we are glad to use your own template.

What file formats do you need to start?

A photo, sketch, or reference sample is enough to begin. If you have technical files, we work with STEP, IGES, DWG, and PDF. Our team can also create the drawings from scratch based on your brief.

What if the T1 sample does not meet expectations?

We revise the mold and produce T2 samples. Minor dimensional corrections are routine at this stage and are handled before mass production begins — you approve the final sample before we run your order.
